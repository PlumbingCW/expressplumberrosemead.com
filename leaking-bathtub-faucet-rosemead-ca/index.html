Leaking Bathtub Faucet: Causes and Fixes for Rosemead Homes

A bathtub faucet that drips wastes water and stains the tub. The fix depends on the faucet type, and it is often simple.

By the Rosemead Express Plumbing team. Published 2026-11-16.

Start by identifying the faucet type

Tub faucets come in several designs. Single handle faucets usually use a cartridge or ball. Two handle faucets use washers or cartridges in each handle.

Knowing the type tells you which part wears out, and it tells you what to bring to the hardware store.

Code and standards

The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), covers fixture connections and backflow protection, which is part of why valve repairs inside a wall should be left to a plumber.

Where the leak shows up

A moisture warning

Check for damage

A leaking tub faucet that runs down behind the wall can rot framing and feed mold. If the wall around the tub feels soft or looks stained, treat it as more than a drip.

A local note on hard water and older fixtures

Check your provider's water quality report for hardness. Hard water leaves scale on cartridges and seats, which wears seals faster. Older Rosemead homes, with about 51.6 percent built before 1960 per the City of Rosemead Housing Element, may also have older valves that are hard to find parts for.

Basic repair steps

Shut off the water

Use the fixture shutoff if there is one, or the main valve.

Remove the handle

Take off the cap, unscrew the handle and remove the trim plate.

Pull the cartridge

Use pliers or a puller and note how it faces.

Replace and reassemble

Install the matching cartridge and test before sealing anything.

If the valve body is corroded or the handle will not come off, stop and call a plumber. Forcing parts can crack pipe inside the wall.

Single handle and two handle faucets

A single handle tub faucet usually has one cartridge or a ball assembly behind the handle. When it wears out, water can pass through even when the handle is off, and the spout drips.

A two handle faucet has a separate valve on each side, and each has a stem with a washer or a cartridge. If only the hot side drips, only the hot valve needs repair.

Valves that are hidden in the wall are the harder case. The trim comes off easily, but the valve body is soldered into the supply lines, and replacing it requires access from the other side of the wall.

When to call for help

If you cannot find the right part, if water leaks inside the wall or if the shutoff does not work, call a plumber. Our faucet repair team can fix tub and shower valves, and if the problem is a hidden leak, see leak detection.

Diverters and shower heads

If the shower head drips after you switch from tub to shower, the diverter is probably worn. Some diverters sit in the spout and lift with a knob, and others are part of the valve. A worn spout diverter is easy to replace, and a valve diverter may need a plumber.

If water pours from the spout while the shower is on, the diverter is not sealing and the shower will have low pressure too.

Quick troubleshooting list

  • Drip at the spout when off points to the cartridge or washer.
  • Leak behind the handle points to O rings or packing.
  • Water from the shower head while the tub runs points to the diverter.
  • Stains on the ceiling below point to a leak in the wall or drain.

Match your symptom to the list, shut the water off before any repair and call if the problem is inside the wall.

Parts and brands

Tub faucet parts are specific to the brand and model. Photograph the handle and the trim before you take them off, and bring the old cartridge to the supplier so it can be matched.

If the brand is not marked, look behind the handle for a stamp on the cartridge or the valve body. A plumber who sees many faucet types can often identify the part by sight, and carries common cartridges on the truck.

Protecting the wall and the floor below

A leaking tub faucet can run back into the wall cavity through the opening around the spout. Seal that joint with the proper caulk, not just plain silicone, and make sure the spout fits tight to the wall.

Watch for signs of moisture behind the tub, such as a stained ceiling below, soft drywall or tile that is loose. Those are signs that the leak has been running longer than the drip suggests.
