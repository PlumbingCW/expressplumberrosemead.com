Repiping in Rosemead, CA

Repiping in Rosemead replaces the water supply pipes in your home when old lines keep leaking or no longer deliver clean water. The aim is to stop the cycle of repairs and restore steady pressure.

We assess the pipe type, find the weak points and propose a repiping plan in phases if needed. For a single leak, burst pipe repair may be enough.

Code and standards

NSF/ANSI 61 certification covers materials that touch drinking water, and the California plumbing code, which is based on the uniform plumbing code (upc), lists the pipe types approved for water supply. We confirm that every pipe we install is listed for potable water.

Signs your pipes may need replacing

Repeated leaks

Fixing one pinhole leak only for another to appear soon after.

Discolored water

Brown or rusty water when the tap first runs suggests corroding lines.

Low water pressure

Pipe narrowing from corrosion reduces flow.

Visible corrosion

Green stains on copper or rust on galvanized pipe.

What to do if a pipe fails before the repipe

If a pipe splits while you are waiting for a repipe, close the main shutoff valve at once and open a low faucet to drain the line. Then call us any hour. Do not try to wrap a failing pipe with tape and leave it, because tape is a short term measure at best.

Move belongings away from wet walls and take photos for your insurer. If the water is near electrical outlets or the panel, stay away and do not touch anything electrical.

Why older Rosemead homes may need it

About 51.6 percent of Rosemead homes were built before 1960, according to the City of Rosemead Housing Element. Homes of that age may have galvanized steel or older copper supply lines that are now near the end of their service life.

Each water provider in the area supplies water with its own chemistry, so check your annual water quality report. Corrosive water can shorten copper life.

Living through a repipe

Most repipes are done in stages so you keep some water. Plan on a few hours each day without water and have drinking water and bathroom options ready.

Expect drywall openings along pipe runs. We keep them as small as possible, and many homeowners hire a patch and paint contractor afterward. Clear furniture from the walls we need to open.

Pets and children should stay away from the work area, since cutting and soldering tools are in use.

Partial or whole house repipe

Sometimes one section is the problem, such as a failed run to a bathroom. A partial repipe replaces only that section and can be a sensible choice if the rest of the pipe is sound.

When leaks appear in more than one place, a whole house repipe usually saves money over time and ends the cycle of patching.

Questions we hear before a repipe

Homeowners often ask whether new pipe will change water taste. Fresh copper or PEX typically gives clean water once the lines are flushed, and removing corroded steel often improves clarity.

They also ask about resale. Updated supply lines are a plus when buyers look at an older house, and a permit with a final inspection documents the work.
