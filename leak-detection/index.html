Leak detection in Rosemead, CA

Leak detection in Rosemead is about locating a hidden leak with the least possible damage. We use listening gear, thermal imaging and pressure tests to narrow down where water is escaping, then confirm before opening a wall or floor.

Many leaks are repairable once found, and the earlier we find them the smaller the repair. If the leak is under a concrete floor, see slab leak repair.

Code and standards

According to the EPA WaterSense program, household leaks can waste nearly 10,000 gallons of water a year. That is why we suggest checking the meter as soon as a bill looks wrong.

Suspect an active leak? Do this first

Water running through a ceiling or flooding a floor is an emergency. Shut off the main valve and call us any hour.

Find the main shutoff

Close it fully if water is spreading. In many homes it sits near the street side or where the line enters the house.

Turn off the power if wet

If water reaches outlets or the panel, do not touch them and call an electrician too.

Take photos

Pictures of stains and meter readings help with an insurance claim.

Catch what drips

Buckets and towels limit damage while we are on the way.

Reading the meter to confirm a leak

Turn off every fixture and appliance, then look at your water meter. If the small leak indicator spins or the dial moves with everything off, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and your fixtures.

A high bill is the other common clue. Rosemead has several water providers, so check your own bill for the provider name and compare this month to a normal one. A jump with no change in how you use water often points to a leak, a running toilet or an irrigation fault.

Where leaks hide

Behind walls and ceilings

Stains, bubbling paint and a musty smell show water moving through framing. Thermal imaging helps trace it.

Under the house or slab

Warm spots on floors and the sound of running water at night can mean a line under concrete.

In irrigation lines

Soggy patches in the yard, low sprinkler pressure and a high bill point to buried lines.

In pools and spas plumbing

Water loss without evaporation often traces to buried return or supply lines.

Leaks under the house

Many older homes in the San Gabriel Valley sit on a raised foundation with a crawlspace beneath the floor. A supply or drain leak down there can run for months because nobody sees it.

Clues include a musty smell, floors that feel soft or cold, a meter that moves with everything off, and damp insulation or standing water when someone looks through the access panel. Do not crawl in alone if you see standing water near wiring. Tell us what you found and we will explain what happens next.
