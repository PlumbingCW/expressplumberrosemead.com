Tankless water heaters in Rosemead, CA

A tankless water heater in Rosemead heats water as it flows instead of storing it, so you do not run out during back to back showers. The unit mounts on a wall and takes far less room than a tank.

We install new tankless units, convert from tank systems and repair existing ones. For a straight tank swap, see water heater installation.

Code and standards

The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the manufacturer installation instructions control gas sizing, venting and clearances for a tankless unit. We check both before we quote.

What has to be right before it goes in

Gas line capacity

Tankless burners need more gas at once than most tanks, so the line size matters.

Venting

Many units need special vent material and a termination that meets code.

Water supply

Flow rate and pressure decide how many fixtures can run together.

Electrical outlet

Even gas models need power for the controls.

Tank and tankless units trade off space, upkeep and venting needs, so the right choice depends on your home.

Hard water and descaling

Rosemead has several water providers, so hardness varies by address. Check your provider's water quality report, because scale builds up inside the heat exchanger and lowers performance. Descaling once a year is a common recommendation for hard water, and a softener can reduce the load.

Gas safety and venting in a Rosemead home

Gas appliances need clear venting and tight connections. If you ever smell gas, leave the house and call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 or 911 before anything else. We test every gas joint on a new installation and check the vent termination for clearance.

Southern California homes often have the water heater in a garage or an exterior closet, which can make venting runs straightforward. Interior closets and attics are tougher and may need a different vent type.

Common tankless problems we repair

Error codes

We read the code and test the sensor or valve behind it.

Cold water sandwich

Hot, cold, then hot again usually points to flow or sensor settings.

Low flow

Scale or a clogged filter screen can restrict water through the unit.

No ignition

Gas supply, venting or a flame sensor can be the cause.

Maintenance schedule

A yearly descale and a check of the inlet screen keep a tankless heater running efficiently. If you have hard water, you may need to do it more often. Ask the installer for the maker schedule and keep a record of each service.

We install isolation valves with every tankless unit, so flushing is simple and does not require shutting down the whole house.
