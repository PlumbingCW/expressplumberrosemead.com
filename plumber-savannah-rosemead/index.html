A historic name in northern Rosemead

Savannah was a settlement in the 1850s, long before Rosemead became a city in 1959. Settlers farmed the land, a Southern Pacific rail depot carried the name, and the community was later absorbed by Rosemead.

The name survives in a few places that residents still use. Savannah Elementary School sits at 3720 North Rio Hondo Avenue and belongs to the Rosemead Elementary School District. Savannah Memorial Park, a California Historical Landmark, is at 9263 Valley Boulevard, and part of it lies in El Monte.

How we define the area

The city of Rosemead does not publish a map of named neighborhoods, so Savannah has no official boundary. We use the school and the park above as anchors and do not claim exact streets. If you are not sure whether your home is part of Savannah, it does not matter. Give us your street address and we will help with the problem either way.

What we know about homes here, and what we do not

We do not have housing data for Savannah alone, and we will not guess. At the city level, the 2020 census counted about 14,900 homes in Rosemead. About 47 percent of occupied homes are owner occupied and about 53 percent are rented.

That mix matters when something breaks. If you rent, your landlord is usually responsible for pipes, drains and the water heater, so tell them first. If you own, you are responsible for the plumbing on your property from the house to the point where it meets the public line.

The year your home was built tells you more than the neighborhood name. The Los Angeles County Assessor lists the year built for most properties, and you can look yours up online. Homes with older pipes and drain lines may need more attention, but age alone does not prove a problem, which is why we inspect before we recommend anything.

Who handles what when the problem is outside your home

Sewage in the street or a blocked public sewer is a different problem from a clog inside your house. Los Angeles County Public Works maintains the public sewers in Rosemead and takes urgent reports at 800-675-4357.

Water service in Rosemead comes from more than one provider, and the supplier depends on your address. Your water bill shows who serves you. If water is flowing in the street or at the meter, call your supplier first. If the problem is on your side of the meter, call a plumber.

Not sure which side it is? A camera look can show whether a blockage is on your private line or beyond it.

Before the plumber arrives

A few steps limit damage while you wait. They apply to any home.

Find the main shutoff

Turn it clockwise to stop a leaking or burst pipe. If you cannot find it, tell us when you call.

Stop using drains

If waste is backing up, stop flushing and running water until the line is cleared.

Shut off the water heater

For a leak near the tank, turn off the power or gas and close the cold supply valve.

Photograph the damage

Pictures help if you decide to contact your insurer.

Code and standards

California plumbing work follows the California Plumbing Code, which adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code. Permits for larger work such as water heater replacement or repiping come from the city building office. Ask whoever does the work whether a permit applies.
