Plumbing Repair Estimates: How to Compare Quotes in Rosemead

Two quotes for the same repair can look very different. Here is how to read them and what to ask before you say yes.

By the Rosemead Express Plumbing team. Published 2026-11-16.

What a good estimate includes

Scope of work

A clear description of what will be done and where.

Parts and materials

Named parts and brands, so you can compare.

Labor and fees

How labor is charged and whether any trip or after hours fee applies.

Exclusions

What is not included, such as wall repair or permits.

Code and standards

According to the Contractors State License Board, you can verify any California contractor license online at cslb.ca.gov before you sign.

Questions to ask

Is the price firm?

Ask whether it can change and under what conditions.

Who pulls permits?

The plumber or you, and what it costs.

What is warranted?

Ask for labor and parts warranty terms.

Who is on site?

Ask whether the work is done by the company or a subcontractor.

Check any contractor's license

California license lookup

The Contractors State License Board lets anyone look up a contractor license by name or number at cslb.ca.gov. Verify the license status, any complaint history the board posts and whether the business carries a bond. Do this for any contractor before work begins.

Reading the parts and labor lines

Labor is usually the larger share of a plumbing repair, and companies charge it in different ways. Some bill by the hour, some use flat rates for common tasks and some add a trip fee to the first hour. None of these is wrong, but you should know which one you are looking at.

Parts should be named so you can check them. A line that just says "materials" tells you nothing, while "ceramic disc cartridge for the named faucet model" lets you compare quotes and check the part online.

If a quote lumps everything into one number, ask for it to be broken out. A company that is confident in its price will not mind.

Emergencies are different

When water is flooding a room, you cannot collect three quotes. Shut off the water, call a company you trust and ask for a clear price before work starts. After the emergency, you can plan any larger work at a slower pace.

How to compare two quotes fairly

Lay the two estimates side by side and check that they cover the same scope. One plumber may include wall patching, permit fees and cleanup while the other leaves them out. The cheaper number is not cheaper if you then pay a second contractor for the patch.

Look at warranty terms next. A longer labor warranty on a repair can be worth more than a small difference in price. Ask what the warranty does not cover, such as damage from freezing or from changes made by someone else.

Finally, think about the person you are dealing with. A dispatcher who explains the work plainly and answers your questions without pressure usually reflects the company you are hiring.

Quick checklist before you approve work

  • The scope of work is written and matches what you discussed.
  • Parts, labor and any fees are listed or explained.
  • You know who is responsible for permits and cleanup.
  • Warranty terms for parts and labor are stated.
  • The contractor license can be verified on the state board website.

If any item is missing, ask for it before work starts. A short delay is cheaper than a dispute later.

Keeping records

Save every estimate, invoice and warranty document in one folder. If a repair fails or a dispute comes up, those papers decide the outcome. Take photos of the problem before and after the work too, since they show what changed.

If the job involves a permit, keep the permit and the final inspection record. They matter when you sell the home, because buyers and their inspectors often ask for them.

Why estimates change on the job

Hidden conditions are the most common reason a price moves. Opening a wall can reveal corroded pipe, a failed shutoff or old repairs. A plumber should stop, show you the problem and explain the new scope before continuing.

If a plumber raises the price without showing you why, ask for the reason in writing. Honest changes come with an explanation, and you keep the right to say no and pay only for the work already done.
