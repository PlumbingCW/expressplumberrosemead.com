Code and standards

The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), sets baseline rules for traps, interceptors and fixtures in commercial buildings. Local agencies add their own grease rules, so we ask what applies to your business.

What to have ready when you call

A few details help the dispatcher send the right person. Tell us the business type, the address, which fixtures or lines are affected and whether the problem started suddenly or has been building.

If you rent, have your landlord or property manager contact handy. Many leases split responsibility for pipes inside the unit from those in shared walls and shared sewer lines, and a quick call to them can save time.

We keep our written scope simple, so a manager or owner can approve work quickly and the job does not stall.

Preventing recurring problems

Restaurants and food service shops get the most repeat calls, almost always from grease. Grease traps and interceptors need regular cleaning, and floor drains in prep areas collect food debris that should be screened out.

Retail and office buildings see more fixture wear. Replacing flush valves, supply lines and aerators on a schedule is cheaper than an emergency visit, and a yearly camera check of shared lines catches problems early.
