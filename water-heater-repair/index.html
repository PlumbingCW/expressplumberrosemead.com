Water heater repair in Rosemead, CA

A cold shower is the usual first sign that you need water heater repair in Rosemead. The cause is often a small part, such as a thermostat, heating element, igniter or thermocouple, and not the whole tank.

We test before we replace anything, so you pay for the part that failed. If the tank itself is leaking, we say so right away and point you to water heater replacement.

Code and standards

The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), requires a working temperature and pressure relief valve with a proper discharge pipe. We check both on every water heater repair visit.

No hot water tonight? What to check

Gas smell means leave and call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 or 911. If there is no smell, a few quick checks can help while you wait for us.

Check the breaker

On an electric heater, a tripped breaker is the most common reason for no hot water at all.

Look at the pilot

On a gas heater, see whether the pilot flame is lit. Do not keep relighting it if it goes out repeatedly.

Check for leaks

Dry the base and look for fresh drips. Water at the bottom points to a tank problem.

Do not raise the thermostat

Turning it far up will not fix a failed part and can create a scalding risk.

Common failures and what they mean

Lukewarm water only

Often a failed lower heating element or a sediment layer insulating the burner.

Popping or rumbling noises

Scale and sediment in the bottom of the tank trap steam bubbles.

Pilot keeps going out

A worn thermocouple or poor draft can shut the gas off.

Water leaking from the relief valve

This can mean high pressure or a worn valve and should be checked promptly.

How we diagnose the problem

We check power or gas supply, test the thermostat and elements or burner, and look at the venting and relief valve. On electric units we measure the elements with a meter, and on gas units we look at the flame quality and the draft.

A dripping valve can point to high pressure, a worn seat or an overheating tank, so it deserves a prompt look.

Maintenance that prevents repairs

A yearly flush clears sediment that makes elements fail and tanks rumble. Test the relief valve lever once a year, and keep the area around a gas heater clear of storage so air can reach the burner.

Check the strapping too. California requires seismic bracing, and loose straps are easy to fix. If the heater is more than about 10 years old, ask us for an honest age and condition check.
