Kitchen Plumbing Installation: A Planning Guide for Rosemead Homes

A kitchen remodel touches supply lines, drains, shutoffs and often the gas line. A little planning prevents costly surprises.

By the Rosemead Express Plumbing team. Published 2026-11-16.

Start with the layout

The sink, dishwasher and disposal all share a drain, and moving any of them changes the pipe. A new layout may need drain lines rerouted and vented properly, while keeping the sink in place usually keeps work simple.

Decide on appliances before the plumbing is finished, because each has its own connection needs.

Code and standards

The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), sets trap, vent and connection rules for kitchen sinks, dishwashers and disposals.

What a typical install includes

Supply lines and shutoffs

New stop valves for each fixture so you can shut off water locally.

Sink and faucet

Mounting, sealing and connecting the faucet and drain.

Disposal and dishwasher

Electrical, drain and air gap or high loop connections.

Pressure test

Checking every joint under pressure before closing up.

Gas safety first

If you smell gas

Leave the house, do not use switches or phones inside and call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 or 911. Gas work in a kitchen remodel should always be tested and inspected.

Older homes bring surprises

About 51.6 percent of Rosemead homes were built before 1960 per the City of Rosemead Housing Element. Older kitchens may have galvanized supply lines, narrow drains or unvented fixtures.

Opening the wall often reveals these. Budget time to repair or replace old lines, see repiping.

Choosing fixtures that last

Solid brass faucets with ceramic cartridges, stainless sinks with sound insulation and disposals sized to your use hold up better. Match hole counts and sink dimensions before you buy.

Our plumbing installation team can check the existing connections and tell you whether your fixtures fit.

The order of work in a remodel

Plumbing comes in two stages. The rough in happens when walls are open and cabinets are not yet set, and it covers drain and supply lines, vents and shutoff valves. The finish stage comes after cabinets and countertops, when fixtures are set and connected.

Inspections, when required, happen between the stages. An inspector may want to see the open lines under pressure before the walls are closed, so do not let the drywall crew start until the plumbing passes.

Giving your plumber the sink and faucet specifications early keeps the rough in accurate. A faucet that needs a drilled hole in a place the plumber did not expect can delay the finish stage.

Coordinating the trades

Plumbing rough in comes before cabinets and countertops, and the final connections come after. Give your plumber the countertop and sink template early, since cutouts must match.

We work with remodelers in Rosemead and Temple City, and we can schedule rough in and finish visits around other trades.

Dishwashers, disposals and filters

A dishwasher needs a hot water supply, a drain connection and power. The drain hose is usually routed through a high loop under the counter or an air gap to keep dirty water from flowing back into the machine.

A disposal shares the sink drain and needs its own power. If you plan a filter or an instant hot tap under the sink, tell your plumber, because each adds a connection and takes cabinet space.

Under sink space is limited, so plan the layout of the stop valves and filters so you can still reach the trap and the shutoffs.

Quick checklist for your remodel

  • Choose the sink, faucet and appliances before rough in.
  • Confirm shutoff valves for every fixture.
  • Ask about permits for any layout change.
  • Test every connection and look under the sink after a day.

A short list kept with your remodel plans saves arguments later and helps each trade know what the others need.

Budget for what is behind the wall

Old kitchens often hide surprises, such as corroded shutoffs, patched drains or lines that do not meet current code. Set aside a margin in your budget for plumbing changes you cannot see yet.

The best way to limit surprises is a pre-demolition look by a plumber. They can check the visible pipes, test the shutoffs and tell you whether the existing lines can serve the new layout.

Choosing a plumber for the project

Look for a plumber who asks about your layout and appliances before quoting, and who explains what will be done at rough in and finish. Ask whether they pull permits and arrange inspections.

Confirm that the license is active on the state license board website and ask how they handle surprises behind the walls. A clear plan for those moments is a good sign.

Avoiding callbacks after the remodel

Once the kitchen is finished, run every fixture and look under the sink after twenty four hours for slow drips. A new connection that is slightly loose will often show moisture the next day, not during the install.

Keep a record of the faucet model, the installation date and the shutoff locations. It makes any later repair faster and helps with warranty claims.
