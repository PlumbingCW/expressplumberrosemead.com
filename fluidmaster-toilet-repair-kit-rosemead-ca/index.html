Fluidmaster Toilet Repair Kit: Fixing a Running Toilet in Rosemead

A running toilet is one of the cheapest plumbing problems to fix yourself. Here is how a Fluidmaster repair kit works and where it stops being enough.

What a Fluidmaster kit replaces

Fluidmaster is a well known brand that sells universal toilet repair kits. A typical kit includes a fill valve, which controls how much water enters the tank, and a flapper, which seals the opening between the tank and the bowl.

Those two parts cause most running toilet complaints. When the flapper wears out, water leaks into the bowl and the fill valve keeps topping off the tank, which is why you hear the toilet cycle on its own.

Code and standards

According to the EPA WaterSense program, labeled toilets use 1.28 gallons per flush or less, so a running toilet wastes water quickly.

First confirm the problem

Before you buy anything, identify whether the flapper or the fill valve is the cause. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and wait fifteen minutes without flushing. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper is leaking.

If water keeps flowing into the overflow tube or the tank will not stop filling, the fill valve or float is the likely cause.

How to install a repair kit

Shut off the supply

Turn the valve behind the toilet clockwise, flush and sponge out the remaining water.

Remove the old fill valve

Disconnect the supply line and the locknut under the tank, then lift the valve out.

Set the new valve

Adjust the height so the top sits at the level marked in the instructions, then tighten the nut by hand.

Replace the flapper

Unhook the old flapper, attach the new one to the pegs and connect the chain with a little slack.

Turn the water on slowly, check every connection for drips and flush a few times. Set the water level about an inch below the top of the overflow tube.

When a kit is not the answer

A kit will not fix a cracked tank, a leak at the base of the toilet or a deteriorated flush valve seat. Water around the base often means a failed wax ring, and that needs the toilet lifted and reset.

If you would rather have it done, see toilet repair. For leaks into the floor, also consider leak detection, because a hidden leak can look like a running toilet on your bill.

Mistakes that make a repair fail

The most common mistake is overtightening plastic nuts, which can crack the tank or the fill valve shank. Hand tight plus a small additional turn is enough.

Another is setting the fill valve too high, which lets water spill into the overflow tube and keeps the toilet running. Follow the height marks in the instructions and check the water line against the overflow tube.

A third is forgetting to flush the supply line before connecting the new valve. Debris can lodge in the valve and cause a slow leak. Open the supply briefly into a bucket first.

Quick checklist for a toilet repair

  • Shut off the supply and flush to empty the tank.
  • Replace the fill valve and flapper together.
  • Set the water level an inch below the overflow tube.
  • Check every connection for drips after the swap.

If the toilet still runs after the repair, the flush valve seat may be worn or the tank cracked, and that is a job for a plumber.

Know your toilet

Open the tank and look at the parts before you shop. Check whether the flapper is a two inch or three inch size, whether the fill valve rises from the bottom of the tank and which way the handle arm points.

Taking a photo of the inside of your tank to the store helps the staff match parts. A kit made for the wrong flush valve size will leak no matter how well you install it.
