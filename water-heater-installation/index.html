Water heater installation in Rosemead, CA

Water heater installation in Rosemead starts with the right size and fuel type for your household. We look at how many people use hot water, where the unit will sit and what gas, venting and electrical connections already exist.

Then we set the tank, connect the supply lines, add the code required safety parts and test the unit before we leave. If you are replacing a failed tank, see water heater replacement.

Code and standards

The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), governs relief valves, venting, combustion air and gas connections for a new heater. Manufacturer instructions apply as well, and we follow both.

What a proper installation includes

Right sizing

We match tank size and recovery to your household so you do not run out or overpay.

Safety valves and piping

A temperature and pressure relief valve with a proper discharge line is part of every install.

Seismic strapping

California requires bracing. We strap the tank to the wall framing.

Venting and gas checks

Gas units need correct venting and a leak check at every joint.

Tank and tankless units trade off space, upkeep and venting needs, so the right choice depends on your home.

Where the unit goes and why it matters

Most Rosemead homes are single-family houses, and many have the heater in a garage or a utility closet. A garage install needs the burner raised or protected from vehicle impact under common code practice, and closets need adequate air. We check this on the visit.

Older homes may also need a new shutoff valve, an expansion tank or updated piping before a modern heater can be connected safely.

What to expect on installation day

Shutoff and drain

We close the water, drain the old tank and disconnect gas or electric safely.

Set and connect

The new unit goes in with new flex connectors, a relief valve and a discharge line.

Brace and test

We strap the tank, fire it up, check venting and test for leaks.

Walkthrough

We show you the shutoffs and the temperature setting before we leave.

Plan on having access to the heater area and the main water shutoff. Move storage away from the unit so the crew can work safely.

Keeping the new heater running well

Set the thermostat at a moderate temperature, flush the tank once a year and test the relief valve lever gently when you do. A yearly look at the anode rod can extend tank life in hard water areas. If you notice a drip, call us early, because small leaks are cheaper to fix.
