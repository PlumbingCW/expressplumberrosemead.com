The commercial street of Rosemead

Garvey Avenue is named for Richard Garvey Sr., who donated part of his land for the road. It became an important link between Los Angeles and the San Gabriel Valley and today runs through Rosemead as a shopping and dining street.

The city's General Plan names Garvey Avenue as a key commercial corridor. One of its policies calls for revitalizing the avenue east of the Southern California Edison easement, and it marks a high intensity commercial area on Garvey between Del Mar and San Gabriel Avenues. The Square, a shopping center on the avenue formerly known as Diamond Square, is one of the better known stops.

Kitchens, grease and drains

Restaurants and bakeries send grease, starch and food scraps down their drains. Grease cools inside the pipe, coats the walls and catches debris until the line is nearly closed.

Many food businesses use a grease trap or interceptor to catch grease before it reaches the sewer. These devices need regular cleaning, and the pumping is normally done by a grease hauler. We help with the drain lines around them. Local sanitation and health rules decide what a business needs, so check with the city before you change anything.

Hydro jetting can clear built up grease from a line more thoroughly than a cable can. A camera inspection afterward shows whether the pipe is in good shape.

Tenants, landlords and shared lines

Many businesses on a commercial street rent their space. Leases often split responsibility for the pipes inside a unit from those in shared walls and shared sewer lines. Check your lease or call your property manager before you book work on a shared line.

When you call us, have the business address, the affected fixtures and a property manager contact ready. If a plumbing failure is stopping business, say so. We will tell you what can be arranged.

Before the plumber arrives

A few steps reduce damage and downtime at any business.

Close the water shutoff

Know where the main shutoff is and who can reach it. Turn it clockwise to stop a leak.

Stop using affected drains

If a floor drain or sink is backing up, stop dumping water into it.

Protect stock and equipment

Move boxes, food and electronics away from wet floors if it is safe to do so.

Note what happened

Write down when it started and which fixtures were involved. It speeds up the diagnosis.

Code and standards

Commercial plumbing in California follows the California Plumbing Code, which adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code. Backflow devices on irrigation, fire and some commercial water lines are commonly tested on a schedule set by the local water supplier, so ask your supplier what applies to your property.
